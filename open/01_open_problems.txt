MHD open problems
=================
Not a ranking. Grouped by shape. Speculative hive readings are marked [speculative].
A spectral toy (incompressible vorticity MHD, plus cmhd bones) can poke some of
these and cannot poke others. That split is in 03_hive_map.txt.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Regularity / singularity  (the Navier–Stokes cousin)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1. Global regularity of 3D incompressible MHD
Concept: same Clay-shaped question as Navier–Stokes, with an extra field B.
The viscous, resistive, incompressible system on T^3 or R^3 is locally well-posed
in H^s (s > 1/2). Global smooth solutions exist in 2D (Sermange–Temam). In 3D
it is open whether a smooth solution can blow up in finite time.

  \partial_t u + (u·∇)u = −∇p + (B·∇)B + ν Δu
  \partial_t B + (u·∇)B = (B·∇)u + η ΔB
  ∇·u = ∇·B = 0

Plot: max|ω|(t) and max|J|(t) on a forced HIT / Orszag–Tang run. If both stay
bounded the solution is still smooth. That does not prove the theorem.

A2. Ideal MHD / Euler–MHD finite-time current sheets
Concept: with ν=η=0, can a smooth field fold into a true current sheet in finite time?
Caflisch–Klapper–Steele extended Beale–Kato–Majda: the solution stays smooth on [0,T]
if both vorticity and current stay integrable in time,

  \int_0^T ( ‖ω(t)‖_∞ + ‖J(t)‖_∞ ) dt  <  ∞.

Open whether a real blowup happens, and whether current can spike while vorticity
stays mild (the hive BKM-sheet sentence: if vorticity BKM stays finite while max|J|
spikes, the sheet is dissipative). 2D ideal MHD can form exponential current growth;
3D is unsettled.

Plot: log max|J| vs log max|ω| on decaying Orszag–Tang / Crow. A vertical climb
in |J| at bounded |ω| is the sheet signature.

A3. Velocity-only regularity criteria
Concept: numerics keep saying the velocity, not B, is the dangerous field.
Several papers give BKM/Serrin-type conditions that mention only u (or only ω),
with no assumption on B. It is still open whether a true 3D MHD singularity can
form from the magnetic field alone.

Plot: ratio max|J| / max|ω| through a thinning event. If |J| leads and |ω| does
not follow, that is the “magnetic-only” shape.

A4. Hall-MHD and electron-MHD blowup
Concept: adding the Hall term J×B / (ne) changes the stretching. There are
explicit infinite-energy blowups in related models; they do not settle finite-energy
3D Hall-MHD. Hive: mode="hall" is LIVE beside mhd (Venus Ohm,
E = −u×B + ηJ + (d_i/n) J×B, n=1 default; d_i=0 matches mhd).
ES-LHDI stays parked.

Plot: same max|ω|, max|J| with hall on vs off.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. Cascades, Onsager, helicity
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

B1. Onsager-type energy cascade in MHD
Concept: energy can reach arbitrarily small scales only if the fields are rough
enough. Aluie 2017 (1701.08692, already in Merge/01): for weak ideal MHD,

  σ_u ≤ 1/3   or   σ_u + 2 σ_b ≤ 1

is required for a persistent energy flux Π_u + Π_b as ℓ→0. Cross-helicity swaps
the roles of σ_u and σ_b. Whether real MHD turbulence sits on the rough side
of this threshold at every Re is open (and is a measurement, not a theorem).

Plot: Hölder proxies σ_u(p), σ_b(p) from structure functions vs filter scale ℓ.
Hive residual R_σ^E is exactly this certificate.

B2. Magnetic-helicity forward-cascade no-go
Concept: magnetic helicity cannot dump through a local inertial cascade if the
fields stay in L^3. Aluie theorem: if ‖u‖_3 and ‖B‖_3 stay finite then

  lim_{ℓ→0} B_ℓ · ε_ℓ  =  0.

Helicity can still wormhole non-locally (AE10). A forward dissipative helicity
cascade would require exploding third-order moments. Whether decaying HIT
ever violates that is open at accessible Re.

Plot: Π_h(ℓ) = −2 ⟨B_ℓ · ε_ℓ⟩ vs ℓ, together with discrete L^3 norms.
Vanishing Π_h at finite L^3 is the topological certificate.

B3. W is conversion, not a cascade across ℓ
Concept: the resolved stretch

  W_ℓ = (1/4π) B_{ℓ,i} S_{ℓ,ij} B_{ℓ,j}

appears with opposite sign in the kinetic and magnetic large-scale budgets, so
it swaps energy at the same filter scale. It is not an inter-scale flux.
Locked by Aluie; the leftover hive mismatch was 15× vs 4× current-weighted
⟨B:S⟩ pile-up (mask / Poynting / weighting), closed as a hunt not a new identity.

Plot: Π_u, Π_b, and W_ℓ on the same ℓ. Stretch pieces should cancel.

B4. Weak uniqueness / convex integration
Concept: for Euler, convex integration built wild non-unique weak solutions
below the Onsager exponent. MHD analogues exist in the literature; which
exponent is sharp for MHD, and whether they describe any physical cascade,
is open.

Plot: none on the toy. Math paper, not a run.

C. Turbulence phenomenology
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

C1. Spectral slope: −5/3 vs −3/2
Concept: Goldreich–Sridhar (critical balance) wants Kolmogorov k_⊥^{−5/3}.
Boldyrev (dynamic alignment) wants k_⊥^{−3/2}. Beresnyak argued alignment
saturates and the asymptotic slope is −5/3. Schekochihin 2020 review treats
alignment as intermittency, not a slope changer, and then tearing takes over
at small scales. A 2026 anisotropic-simulation paper claims critical balance
and scale-dependent alignment are both violated at large mean field.
Still not community-closed.

Plot: E(k_⊥) compensated by k^{5/3} and k^{3/2} on a strong-B_0 run.
Do not retune hyper_kcut to pick a slope.

C2. Tearing-mediated cascade
Concept: aligned current sheets in the inertial range become tearing-unstable
before they hit viscous/resistive scales, so the cascade itself becomes a
reconnection cascade (Mallet–Loureiro–Schekochihin). Whether this is the
generic dissipation route at large Re, Rm, or only a strong-guide-field story,
is open.

Plot: sheet aspect ratio L/a vs local Lundquist; D(k)/E_J(k) peak (Maron
hyper_kcut sits there, not at 0.5).

C3. Imbalanced turbulence (cross-helicity)
Concept: z^+ and z^− amplitudes unequal. The weaker Elsässer field is not
a passive slave; how the cascade time and residual energy scale with imbalance
is still argued. Decaying imbalanced MHD reconnects its way toward balance
in some pictures (Hosking–Schekochihin).

Plot: E^+(k)/E^−(k) and residual energy E_r = E_m − E_k vs k.

C4. Residual energy
Concept: magnetic energy often exceeds kinetic at small scales even in
balanced MHD. Why, and whether it is a dynamo leftover, a sheet effect, or
an alignment geometry, is open.

Plot: E_b(k) − E_u(k) through the inertial range.

C5. Decaying MHD: 1/Re heat vs leak
Concept: Linkmann–Berera–Goldstraw (1701.01086, Merge/09): the dimensionless
dissipation

  C_ε = ε L_+ /(z_+^2 z_−) + ε L_− /(z_−^2 z_+)

has a finite-Re expansion C_ε = C_{ε,∞} + C/R_− + D/R_−^2.
A few-percent ΔE_tot at working Re is physical viscoresistive heat, not a
fake leak, once ε is split viscous vs Ohmic. Hive locked the Crow ~6% floor
this way. Open: how universal C_{ε,∞} really is once helicity and forcing
change, and how the split sits on sheets vs tubes.

Plot: C_ε vs R_−; f_visc = ε_kin/ε; co-location of Ohmic with high-|J|.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. Dynamo
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1. Fast kinematic dynamo
Concept: can a smooth flow grow magnetic energy at a rate independent of η
as Rm→∞? Slow dynamos vanish as 1/log Rm or worse. Fast dynamo needs
Lagrangian chaos plus a stretch-fold-shear geometry. Existence of a fast
dynamo for a smooth, incompressible, 3D periodic flow at infinite Rm is a
classical open problem (Childress–Gilbert). Numerical growth rates look
fast; a proof does not exist.

Plot: γ(Rm) = d log E_b / dt vs Rm on a prescribed chaotic u. Saturation
is a different problem (D2).

D2. Small-scale dynamo saturation
Concept: Kazantsev kinematic growth at k_η, then Lorentz back-reaction
stops the stretch. The saturated spectrum, the role of reconnection in
setting the cutoff, and whether a large-scale field can be built from a
small-scale dynamo (mean-field closure) are open. Schekochihin’s review
ties the saturated state to tearing again.

Plot: E_b(k) during kinematic growth vs after Lorentz saturates.
Mean B vs fluctuation b_rms.

D3. Large-scale / mean-field dynamo
Concept: α-effect, inverse cascade of magnetic helicity, catastrophic
quenching at large Rm. Whether a closed mean-field theory exists that
does not cheat on magnetic helicity flux is still contested.
[speculative hive braid: helicity no-go (B2) is the same topological
protection that makes α-quenching hard.]

Plot: large-scale |⟨B⟩| growth vs magnetic-helicity flux through the box.

E. Reconnection
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

E1. Onset
Concept: energy builds in a thick sheet; release is sudden. What actually
triggers? Electron tearing after external thinning, ion-scale B_z-hump
flux-release, plasmoid-on-a-forming-sheet (least-time, not Sweet–Parker),
kink of a flux rope, tether-cutting / breakout in flares. Magnetotail
simulations still form X-lines too close to the box edge compared with
spacecraft. Solar: is Alfvénic motion the cause or the result?

Plot on the toy: thinning rate 1/τ vs tearing growth γ. Onset when γ > 1/τ.
Do not start from a Sweet–Parker sheet; form it.

E2. Rate: why ~0.1, and is it universal?
Concept: Sweet–Parker is slow (~ S^{−1/2}) and is never reached at large S
because plasmoids disrupt the sheet. Collisionless and plasmoid-mediated
rates both sit near 0.1 v_A. Liu et al. argue a geometric maximum of that
order. Whether 0.1 is a theorem, a 2D accident, or a 3D/turbulent number
is open. LV99 stochastic reconnection is a competing 3D picture
(turbulence sets thickness; plasmoids set length).

Plot: reconnection rate v_in / v_A vs Lundquist S, and vs island number.
Reconnected flux Φ(t) — Aethon was asked to add this; it is the live meter.

E3. 3D plasmoid vs turbulent reconnection
Concept: 2D plasmoid chains are understood. 3D: flux ropes kink, turbulence
self-generates from the reconnection (2026 SGTR papers), EDRs come out
laminar or turbulent with no clean switch. Outstanding (Nakamura et al.
2407.09670): why some electron diffusion regions are laminar and others
are not; whether electron-only reconnection is a finite-lifetime stage
before ions couple, or a scale-limited turbulent object.

Plot: none fully on a spectral periodic box without a Harris sheet.
Orszag–Tang / Brio–Wu show the compressible cousin, not this.

E4. Particle acceleration and energy partition
Concept: where does the released magnetic energy go — ions vs electrons,
thermal vs nonthermal? Magnetotail: ion enthalpy wins near the IDR;
nonthermal tails exist even in quiet plasma sheet. Flares: nonthermal
electrons can rival thermal. Relativistic reconnection can make hard
power laws. No common closure. The fluid toy cannot do this; it is
listed so it is not mistaken for an I_leak problem.

Plot: not on the spectral MHD toy.

E5. Compressible / shock-reconnection coupling
Concept: reconnection outflows shock; shocks make reconnection; spectral
MHD rings at discontinuities. Hive patch 8 (Brio–Wu) locked “waves exist,
no NaN, Gibbs is the scheme, no WENO this week.” Open: when does ringing
destroy the Riemann structure vs just decorate it; when must one leave
spectral methods.

Plot: min ρ, max |div B|, I_leak on Brio–Wu before wrap. Success is no NaN.

F. Sheets, dissipation, constraints
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

F1. Current-sheet morphology: sheets vs tubes vs nanoflares
Concept: Zhdankin census — dissipation sits in coherent current sheets
with a power-law size distribution (nanoflare language). Hive leftover
after Crow: extra MHD−hydro heat was tube-scale current, not sitting in
the high-|J| mask (only ~23% of Ohmic in the sheets). Open: is that
resolution, a weighting, or the real partition at large Re?

Plot: Ohmic fraction inside a high-|J| mask vs outside; Minkowski
thickness of the mask.

F2. Viscous heating vs Ohmic heating once ∇·u ≠ 0
Concept: on cmhd, ⟨u · ν∇²u⟩ and ⟨2ν S:S⟩ disagree (HAVE 7: about a
factor two on a 1D sound wave, which is the 1D identity, not a bug).
Bulk viscosity flag exists on cmhd (default off). Open in 3D compressible turbulence: how much
of Q is compressive vs solenoidal, and whether a bulk term is ever needed.

Plot: the two viscous expressions vs time on a sound wave and on Brio–Wu.

F3. Divergence-free B
Concept: constrained transport / GLM (Dedner) vs spectral projection.
Hive: e_glm is in I_leak when glm_ch ≠ 0; Dedner −αψ damping is a sink
not in I_leak; OT does not exercise live ψ. Open: when does max|div B|
stay bad enough to need CT, and is I_glm a real leak or a gauge wave.

Plot: max|div B|(t), e_glm, I_glm (the parked one-liner) with glm on.

F4. Magnetic Prandtl number
Concept: Pm = ν/η. Dynamo, sheet thickness, and viscous/Ohmic split all
change with Pm. Most hive runs sit near Pm=1. Astrophysical Pm spans
10^{−5} to 10^{14}. Open: which qualitative pictures survive far from 1.

Plot: f_visc vs Pm on otherwise identical decaying runs.

G. Compressible MHD
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

G1. Existence of shock solutions / uniqueness of MHD Riemann problems
Concept: the MHD Riemann problem is not always unique; intermediate shocks
and compound waves exist. Brio–Wu (γ=2) is the standard 1D test. Spectral
schemes will Gibbs-ring and can go ρ<0. That is the scheme, not a
continuity leak (Venus lock). Open mathematically: which weak solutions
are physical (entropy, viscosity limits).

Plot: ρ(x), B_y(x) at t < t_wrap against the exact Brio–Wu states.
Do not demand a plot match.

G2. Living acoustics on a spectral torus
Concept: primitive u with −∇p/ρ, Qin off, CFL with c_s and v_A. HAVE 5+6
got v_phase/c_s ≈ 1. Open: magnetoacoustic / fast-mode speeds in 3D, and
whether a spectral code can carry a fast shock without a positivity fix.

Plot: phase speed of a 1D magnetosonic wave vs √(c_s² + v_A²).
