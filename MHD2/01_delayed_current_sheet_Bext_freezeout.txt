### 1. Experimental geometry and frozen-out external field

Two exploding carbon wire arrays (8 mm dia., 10 mm tall, eight 0.4 mm rods) driven in parallel at peak current \(\approx400\,\mathrm{kA}\) (240 ns rise) produce radially expanding magnetised plasma flows that advect an anti-parallel reconnecting field
\[
B_{\rm rec}=1.2\pm0.2\,\mathrm{T}.
\]
A Helmholtz pair fills the experimental volume with a uniform external field \(B_{\rm ext}\le2\,\mathrm{T}\) oriented parallel to the reconnecting electric field (i.e., the out-of-plane direction that would normally become a guide field). Diagnostics: side-on laser interferometry (\(\langle n_e L_y\rangle\)), gated XUV, and in-situ B-dot probes.

**Key observation**:
- \(B_{\rm ext}=0\) and \(0.5\,\mathrm{T}\): dense reconnection layer forms at the mid-plane (\(\langle n_e L_y\rangle_{\rm peak}\approx2.5\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}\)).
- \(B_{\rm ext}=2\,\mathrm{T}\): density *void* appears (\(\langle n_e L_y\rangle\approx0.5\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}\) at \(x=0\)); inflow plasma is redirected and confined near the arrays.

**Frozen-in condition** (hydrodynamic versus resistive diffusion timescales):
\[
\tau_{\rm hydro}=\frac{L_{\nabla B}}{v_{\rm in}}\approx\frac{6\,\mathrm{mm}}{50\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}}\approx100\,\mathrm{ns},
\]
\[
\tau_\eta=\frac{L_{\nabla B}^2}{\bar{\eta}}\approx400\,\mathrm{ns}
\]
(Spitzer–Braginskii resistivity at \(T_e\approx10\,\mathrm{eV}\), \(\bar{Z}=4\), \(\ln\Lambda=10\)). Because \(\tau_{\rm hydro}\ll\tau_\eta\) (equivalently \(R_M=V L_{\nabla B}/\bar{\eta}>1\)), the external field cannot diffuse into the incoming plasma and is simply compressed between the two stagnation fronts.

### 2. Pressure balance and magnetic back-pressure

Total pressure is approximately constant across the interaction region:
\[
P_{\rm th}+P_{\rm mag}+P_{\rm kin}\approx\mathrm{const}.
\]
Explicit estimates at \(B_{\rm ext}=2\,\mathrm{T}\):
\[
P_{B,{\rm void}}=\frac{B_{\rm ext}^2}{2\mu_0}\approx2\,\mathrm{MPa},
\]
\[
P_{\rm ram,in}=\rho v_{\rm in}^2=m_i\Bigl(1+\frac1{\bar{Z}}\Bigr)\frac{\langle n_e L_y\rangle}{L_y}v_{\rm in}^2\approx3\,\mathrm{MPa}
\]
(\(m_i=2\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(L_y=20\,\mathrm{mm}\), \(v_{\rm in}\approx50\,\mathrm{km\,s}^{-1}\)). Thermal pressure is negligible (\(\sim5\,\mathrm{kPa}\) at \(T_e=10\,\mathrm{eV}\)). Thus the piled-up external field supplies a back-pressure comparable to the ram pressure, decelerating the inflows and preventing the dense sheet from forming. For the weak-field case \(B_{\rm ext}=0.5\,\mathrm{T}\) one has \(P_B\approx0.1\,\mathrm{MPa}\ll P_{\rm ram}\), so the classic sheet still appears.

In the hive residual layer this is realised by adding an external-guide-field contribution to the total Maxwell stress that is *not* frozen into the plasma velocity:
\[
\mathbf{B}_{\rm tot}=\mathbf{B}_{\rm advected}+\mathbf{B}_{\rm ext}(t),\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}_{\rm tot}=0,\qquad
\partial_t\mathbf{B}_{\rm ext}=0\quad\text{(frozen-out limit)}.
\]
The Lorentz force then acquires an extra \(\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}_{\rm ext}\) term that acts as a pure pressure gradient opposing the inflow.

### 3. Current-sheet aspect-ratio evolution

3-D resistive MHD (GORGON, Eulerian, van-Leer advection, \(100\,\mu\mathrm{m}\) grid) quantifies the delay. The aspect ratio is extracted from the out-of-plane current density \(J_z\):
\[
\delta=\mathrm{FWHM}_x\bigl(\text{Gaussian fit to }J_z\bigr),\qquad
L=\mathrm{FWHM}_y(J_z),\qquad
\frac{\delta}{L}(t).
\]
- \(B_{\rm ext}=0\): sheet forms at \(t\approx140\,\mathrm{ns}\), \(\delta/L\) collapses to \(\sim0.1\) on an Alfvén time.
- \(B_{\rm ext}=0.5\,\mathrm{T}\): formation delayed by \(\approx10\,\mathrm{ns}\).
- \(B_{\rm ext}=1\,\mathrm{T}\): formation delayed by \(\approx40\,\mathrm{ns}\) (to \(180\,\mathrm{ns}\)).
- \(B_{\rm ext}=2\,\mathrm{T}\): no sheet within the simulated window.

After formation the aspect ratio obeys the classic Sweet–Parker scaling
\[
\frac{\delta}{L}\sim S^{-1/2},\qquad S\sim100\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\delta}{L}\sim0.1.
\]
At higher drive current (400 kA) the compression shortens the gradient scale, \(\tau_\eta\) drops to \(\sim10\,\mathrm{ns}\), diffusion occurs, and a true guide-field reconnection layer finally appears with
\[
b=\frac{B_g}{B_{\rm rec}}\approx0.5.
\]

The paper’s magnetic back-pressure is precisely analogous to the polymer hoop / normal-stress contribution already resident in the hive. We therefore close a new residual channel that lets the polymeric stress \(\mathbf{T}\) (or log-conformation \(\boldsymbol{\psi}\)) *further* delay or stabilise the sheet under the same external field.

\[
\rho\bigl(\partial_t\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}\bigr)=-\nabla p+\eta_s\Delta\mathbf{u}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{T}+\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}_{\rm tot},
\]
\[
\mathbf{T}+\lambda\overset{\nabla}{\mathbf{T}}=2\eta_p\mathbf{D},\qquad

\[
P_{\rm poly}=\frac12\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{T}\quad\text{(or the appropriate normal-stress difference)}.
\]
Under the same freeze-out condition the total back-pressure becomes
\[
P_{\rm back}=P_{B,{\rm void}}+P_{\rm poly}.
\]
Consequently the critical external field required to produce a void is lowered, and the \(\delta/L\) collapse is delayed even for moderate \(B_{\rm ext}\). import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Physical constants (SI)
mu0 = 4*np.pi*1e-7
m_i = 2e-26          # kg
Zbar = 4.0
Ly = 0.02            # m
vin0 = 5e4           # m/s

rho = m_i*(1+1/Zbar)*neL_in/Ly

def P_mag(B):
    return B**2/(2*mu0)

def P_ram(vin):
    return rho*vin**2

def P_poly(A_poly, Wi=2.5):
    # schematic normal-stress contribution scaled to hive Wi
    return A_poly * (0.5*rho*vin0**2) * (Wi/(1+Wi))

# 1-D reduced model for inflow deceleration + sheet aspect ratio
# d(vin)/dt = -(P_back - P_ram)/ (rho * L) ; L decreases with compression
def sheet_ode(t, y, Bext, Apoly):
    vin, delta_over_L = y
    Pback = P_mag(Bext) + P_poly(Apoly)
    Pram  = P_ram(vin)
    # deceleration
    dvin = -(Pback - Pram)/(rho * 0.006)   # L_gradB ~ 6 mm
    # aspect-ratio collapse once Pram > Pback (Sweet-Parker-like)
    if Pram > 1.1*Pback:
        d_aspect = -delta_over_L / 20e-9   # ~ Alfvén time scale
    else:
        d_aspect = 0.0
    return [dvin, d_aspect]

def run_sweep(Bext_list=[0.0,0.5,1.0,2.0], Apoly_list=[0.0,1.0,3.0,10.0]):
    t_span = (0, 300e-9)
    t_eval = np.linspace(0, 300e-9, 600)
    results = {}
    for B in Bext_list:
        for A in Apoly_list:
            y0 = [vin0, 1.0]   # start with thick layer
            sol = solve_ivp(sheet_ode, t_span, y0, args=(B,A),
                            t_eval=t_eval, rtol=1e-6)
            # formation time = first time delta/L < 0.2
            idx = np.where(sol.y[1] < 0.2)[0]
            t_form = sol.t[idx[0]] if len(idx)>0 else np.nan
            results[(B,A)] = dict(t=sol.t, vin=sol.y[0],
                                  aspect=sol.y[1], t_form=t_form)
    return results

res = run_sweep()
print("Formation times (ns) vs (Bext [T], Apoly):")
for k,v in res.items():
    print(k, f"{v['t_form']*1e9:.1f}" if not np.isnan(v['t_form']) else "no sheet")
