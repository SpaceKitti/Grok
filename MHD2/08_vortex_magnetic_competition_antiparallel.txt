### 1. Complete mathematical apparatus extracted from the paper

**Incompressible MHD system (constant density \(\rho=1\))**.  
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}&=-\nabla p+(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{b})+\nu\nabla^2\mathbf{u},\\
\partial_t\mathbf{b}&=\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{b})+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf{b},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0,\qquad\mathbf{j}=\nabla\times\mathbf{b}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The vorticity form (used for diagnostics) is
\[
\partial_t\boldsymbol{\omega}=\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}\times\boldsymbol{\omega})+\nu\nabla^2\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla\times(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{b}).
\]

**Initial antiparallel flux tubes**.  
Centerlines (arc-length parameter \(s\)):
\[
\mathbf{c}_\pm(s)=\bigl(\pm s,\,y_0+p f(s)\sin\alpha,\,z_0+p f(s)\cos\alpha\bigr),
\]
with \(y_0=z_0=0\), \(p=0.2\), \(f(s)=\cos s\), \(\alpha=\pm\pi/3\), separation \(\ell_s=1.62\).  
Gaussian cross-section of thickness \(\sigma_{c0}=0.25\):
\[
f_t(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{c0}^2}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{r^2}{2\sigma_{c0}^2}\Bigr).
\]
Vorticity and magnetic fields (identical topology):
\[
\boldsymbol{\omega}(s,r,\theta)=\Gamma_0 f_t(r)\,\mathbf{e}_s,\qquad
\mathbf{b}(s,r,\theta)=\Gamma_{m0} f_t(r)\,\mathbf{e}_s,
\]
where \(\Gamma_0=1\) (fixed) and \(\Gamma_{m0}\) is the control parameter. Fluxes are exactly conserved at infinite Reynolds number:
\[
\Gamma=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS=\Gamma_0,\qquad
\Gamma_m=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS=\Gamma_{m0}.
\]

**Dimensionless numbers**.  
Vortex Reynolds number \(\Rey_v=\Gamma_0/\nu\approx2000\), magnetic Prandtl number \(\Pr_m=\nu/\eta=1\) (hence \(\Rey_m=\Rey_v\)).  
Interaction parameter (Lorentz-to-inertial force ratio):
\[
N_i=\frac{\sigma B^2\ell}{\rho u}=\frac{\Rey_v\Pr_m\Gamma_m^2}{\Gamma_0^2\sigma_c^2}.
\]
In the simulations \(N_i\) is swept from \(12.8\) to \(32000\) by varying \(\Gamma_{m0}\).

**Energy identities**.  
Total electromechanical energy:
\[
E=E_k+E_b=\frac12\int_V\bigl(|\mathbf{u}|^2+|\mathbf{b}|^2\bigr)\,dV,
\]
\[
\frac{dE}{dt}=-\int_V\frac{|\mathbf{j}|^2}{\sigma}\,dV-2\nu\int_V S_{ij}S_{ij}\,dV.
\]
Magnetic energy evolution isolates the Lorentz work:
\[
\frac{dE_b}{dt}=-\int_V(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{b})\cdot\mathbf{u}\,dV-\int_V\frac{|\mathbf{j}|^2}{\sigma}\,dV.
\]

**Three regimes (paper classification)**.  
- **Vortex-dominated joint reconnection** (\(N_i\lesssim51.2\)): Crow instability drives classical vortex reconnection; magnetic flux is passively advected and reconnected, producing a dynamo amplification of \(E_b\).  
- **Instability-triggered cascade** (\(N_i\sim64.8{-}204.8\)): vorticity-driven attraction and magnetic damping oscillate; secondary filaments are spawned; kinetic-energy spectrum develops a clean \(k^{-5}\) range.  
- **Lorentz-induced vortex disruption** (\(N_i\gtrsim8000\)): Lorentz tension suppresses mutual induction, axially aligns the tubes, disrupts the vortex cores and rapidly converts magnetic energy into kinetic energy; no topological reconnection of the magnetic field.

**Instantaneous thickness and local \(N_i\) during reconnection**.  
\[
\sigma_c=\sigma_{c0}\sqrt{2\ln\bigl(1/(1-\kappa\lambda)\bigr)},\qquad
N_i(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda N_{i0}}{-2\ln(1-\kappa\lambda)},\qquad
\max N_i\approx1.27 N_{i0}.
\]
