### 1. Adaptive amalgam-norm monitoring inside every spectral step

The static \(E^r_q\) evaluation is replaced by a fully adaptive, hierarchical monitor that is invoked on every right-hand-side call. At each spectral step the instantaneous enstrophy spectrum and local \(L^r\) mass determine a set of active integer cubes \(\{B_1(k)\}\) whose contribution to the \(\ell^q\) sum exceeds a relative tolerance \(10^{-9}\). Only those cubes are re-evaluated; the remaining lattice points inherit their values from a warm-start cache. The resulting adaptive amalgam norm
\[
\|f\|_{E^r_q}^{\rm adapt}=\Bigl(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_t}\|f\|_{L^r(B_1(k))}^q\Bigr)^{1/q}
\]
(with \(\mathcal{A}_t\) the active set) is injected into the residual as a live diagnostic and as a soft constraint on the dealiasing mask.

**Adaptive local extractor (paper definition generalised)**:
\[
\|f\|_{L^r(B_1(k))}=\Bigl(\int_{B_1(k)}|f|^r\,dx\Bigr)^{1/r},\qquad
\|f\|_{E^r_q}=\bigl\|\{\|f\|_{L^r(B_1(k))}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^3}\bigr\|_{\ell^q}.
\]
Hierarchical refinement: cubes whose local mass exceeds a soft threshold are subdivided into 8 child cubes of side \(1/2\); the process stops at a maximum depth of 4 (corresponding to the Nyquist scale at \(256^3\)).

### 2. Quantitative extraction of the bilinear constant \(C_*\) from the discrete Picard map

The paper’s contraction mapping rests on a bilinear estimate of the form
\[
\|B((v,b),(u,a))\|_{\mathcal{F}_T}\le C_*\|(v,b)\|_{\mathcal{F}_T}\|(u,a)\|_{\mathcal{F}_T},
\]
where \(\mathcal{F}_T\) is the Kato-type space with weight \(t^{1/4}\) in \(E^6_{q_1}\) (critical) or the corresponding subcritical space. The discrete spectral realisation of \(B\) is the Picard residual already resident in the hive. We extract a quantitative numerical value of \(C_*\) by sampling the operator norm of the discrete bilinear map on a ball of radius comparable to the current data size.

**Discrete extraction protocol**:
1. Generate an ensemble of random (or residual-derived) divergence-free pairs \((v^{(i)},b^{(i)})\) normalised to unit \(\mathcal{F}_T\)-norm.
2. Evaluate the spectral bilinear form \(B(v^{(i)},b^{(i)};v^{(j)},b^{(j)})\).
3. Compute the supremum of the ratio
   \[
   C_*^{\rm disc}=\sup_{i,j}\frac{\|B(v^{(i)},b^{(i)};v^{(j)},b^{(j)})\|_{\mathcal{F}_T^{\rm disc}}}{\|v^{(i)},b^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{F}_T^{\rm disc}}\|v^{(j)},b^{(j)}\|_{\mathcal{F}_T^{\rm disc}}}.
   \]
4. The extracted constant is locked into the residual and used as a live guard: if the instantaneous amalgam norm exceeds \(1/(4C_*^{\rm disc})\), the time-stepper automatically switches to the local-energy weak regime.

### 3. Analytic lower bounds on the existence time \(T\) from the subcritical scaling

For subcritical data \(r>3\) the paper guarantees a mild solution on a time interval satisfying
\[
T^{1/2-3/(2r)}+T^{1/2}\lesssim\delta^{-1},\qquad\delta=\|(v_0,b_0)\|_{E^r_q}.
\]
An explicit analytic lower bound is obtained by solving the elementary inequality. Let \(\alpha=1/2-3/(2r)\in(0,1/2)\). Then
\[
T\ge T_{\rm lower}(\delta,r):=\min\Bigl\{\bigl(\tfrac12\delta^{-1}\bigr)^{1/\alpha},\;\bigl(\tfrac12\delta^{-1}\bigr)^2\Bigr\}
\]
is a rigorous (if slightly pessimistic) lower bound. For the critical case \(r=3\) the existence time is global once \(\delta<1/(4C_*)\); otherwise a local existence time of order \(\delta^{-2}\) is recovered from the same scaling.

**Executable lower-bound evaluator**:

def subcritical_T_lower(delta, r=4.0):
    """Analytic lower bound from paper scaling (r>3)."""
    alpha = 0.5 - 1.5 / r
    term1 = (0.5 / delta)**(1.0 / alpha)
    term2 = (0.5 / delta)**2
    return jnp.minimum(term1, term2)

@jit

def critical_T_lower(delta, C_star):
    """Critical (r=3) local existence time when data is not small."""
    if delta < 1.0 / (4.0 * C_star):
        return jnp.inf          # global
    return 1.0 / (delta**2)     # Kato-type local time

    return jnp.clip(T_bound, 1e-4, 1e2)
