### 1. Complete microscopic viscosity tensor (paper equations)

**Stress–strain relation** (including vortical contributions):
\[
\pi_{i,j}=-L^7_{ij,k}\partial_k\phi-L^8_{ij,k}\partial_k T-L^9_{ij,kl}\partial_l u_k.
\]

**Berry-odd viscosity (zero magnetic field, equilibrium distribution only)**:
\[
L^9_{ij,mn}=-\epsilon_{jnl}\int[d\mathbf{k}]\,k_ik_m[\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathbf{k}}]_l\,f^0(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{k}).
\]
Under isotropy (\(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=\varepsilon_{|\mathbf{k}|}\), \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathbf{k}}=\Omega(|\mathbf{k}|)\hat{z}\)) the only non-vanishing components are
\[
L^9_{xx,xy}=L^9_{yx,yy}=-L^9_{xy,xx}=-L^9_{yy,yx}=-\int[d\mathbf{k}]\,k_x^2\Omega_z(\mathbf{k})\,f^0.
\]
These are dissipationless (odd under time reversal) and remain finite at \(T=0\) even inside a band gap.

**Tilted Dirac-cone microscopic model** (paper Eqs. 9–11):
\[
H_s(k_x,k_y)=v k_x\sigma_y-s v k_y(\sigma_x-\alpha)+\Delta\sigma_z,
\]
\[
\varepsilon_{s\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\alpha s v k_y\pm\sqrt{v^2k^2+\Delta^2},
\]
\[
\Omega_{s\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\pm\frac12\frac{s v^2\Delta}{(v^2k^2+\Delta^2)^{3/2}}.
\]
Valley-summed odd viscosity:
\[
L^9_{xx,xy}=-\sum_s\int[d\mathbf{k}]\,k_x^2\Omega_{s+}(\mathbf{k})\,f^0(\varepsilon_{s+}).
\]
(Time-reversal symmetric valleys cancel; a TR-breaking term isolates a finite residue.)

**Phase-space factor and non-equilibrium distribution (finite \(\mathbf{B}\))**:
\[
D_{\mathbf{k}}=\Bigl(1+\frac{e}{\hbar}\mathbf{B}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathbf{k}}\Bigr)^{-1}.
\]
First-order correction:
\[
g_1=\frac{\tau_{ee}}{\hbar}\frac{\partial f^{\rm lab}}{\partial\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}{D_{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot\mathbf{S},
\]
\[
\mathbf{S}=e\mathbf{E}+e\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}+\nabla\mu+\frac{\nabla T}{T}(\varepsilon-\mu)+\hbar(\nabla\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{k}.
\]
Second-order (magnetic-field dependent) correction (paper Eq. 20):
\[
\begin{align*}
g_2&=\frac{e\tau_{ee}}{\hbar^3 D_{\mathbf{k}}}\Bigl[(\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{k}}\Bigl(\frac{\tau_{ee}}{D_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{\partial f^{\rm lab}}{\partial\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr](\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon\cdot\mathbf{S})\\
&+\frac{e\tau_{ee}^2}{\hbar^3 D_{\mathbf{k}}^2}\frac{\partial f^{\rm lab}}{\partial\varepsilon}\Bigl[(\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\bigl((\mathbf{S}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{k}})\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon\bigr)\Bigr]\\
&+\frac{e\tau_{ee}^2}{\hbar^2 D_{\mathbf{k}}^2}\frac{\partial f^{\rm lab}}{\partial\varepsilon}\,\epsilon_{imn}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial k_m}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial k_p}B^n\partial_p u^i.
\end{align*}
\]

**Extended Onsager relations** (paper Eqs. 3a–c):
\[
\begin{align*}
L^3_{i,jk}(\mathbf{B};\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_k\},\{\varepsilon_k\})&=-L^7_{jk,i}(-\mathbf{B};\{-\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{-k}\},\{\varepsilon_{-k}\}),\\
L^6_{k,ji}(\mathbf{B};\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_k\},\{\varepsilon_k\})&=-T\,L^8_{ji,k}(-\mathbf{B};\{-\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{-k}\},\{\varepsilon_{-k}\}),\\
L^9_{ij,kl}(\mathbf{B};\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_k\},\{\varepsilon_k\})&=L^9_{kl,ij}(-\mathbf{B};\{-\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{-k}\},\{\varepsilon_{-k}\}).
\end{align*}
\]

**Finite-\(B\) viscosity tensor** (leading \(\tau_{ee}\) piece + \(\mathcal{O}(\tau_{ee}^2)\) magnetic corrections obtained by substituting \(g_1+g_2\) into the stress integral).

### 1. Full momentum-space adaptive quadrature inside every spectral step

The equilibrium and \(\mathcal{O}(\tau_{ee}^2)\) pieces of \(L^9_{ij,kl}\) require high-accuracy integration over the tilted-Dirac Brillouin zone. Fixed Cartesian grids lose accuracy near the Dirac points and under strong Berry curvature. We therefore replace the previous static quadrature with a fully adaptive, nested Gauss–Kronrod / sparse-grid scheme that is called on every right-hand-side evaluation.

**Adaptive integrator (JAX-compatible, differentiable)**:
\[
L^9_{xx,xy}(\mu,T,\alpha,\Delta,B_z)=\sum_{s=\pm}\int_{\mathbb{R}^2}\!\!d^2k\,k_x^2\,\Omega_{s+}(\mathbf{k})\,f^0(\varepsilon_{s+};\mu,T)\,D_{\mathbf{k}}(B_z)+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{ee}^2).
\]
The integrand is evaluated by a hierarchical adaptive rule that subdivides rectangles until the local error estimate falls below \(10^{-10}\). The same adaptive mesh is reused for the magnetic \(g_2\) corrections that enter the antisymmetric stress.

### 3. Quantitative extraction of the magnetic correction to the Gurzhi length from the locked \(L^9(B)\) surface

In electron hydrodynamics the Gurzhi length is the scale at which viscous momentum diffusion competes with momentum-relaxing scattering:
\[
\lambda_G=\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{nm\gamma_{\rm mr}}},\qquad\eta=\eta_{\rm even}+\eta_{\rm odd}(L^9).
\]
With the microscopic \(L^9(\mathbf{B})\) the effective shear viscosity acquires a magnetic correction
\[
\eta(B)=\eta(0)+\delta\eta(B),\qquad\delta\eta(B)=\frac12\bigl(L^9_{xx,xy}(B)-L^9_{xx,xy}(0)\bigr).
\]
Hence the corrected Gurzhi length is
\[
\lambda_G(B)=\lambda_G(0)\sqrt{1+\frac{\delta\eta(B)}{\eta(0)}}.
\]
We extract the entire surface \(\delta\eta(B_z;\mu,T,\alpha,\Delta)\) by evaluating the adaptive quadrature on a dense \((B_z,\mu)\) grid once per diagnostic interval, then form the relative correction
\[
\delta\lambda_G/\lambda_G=\sqrt{1+\delta\eta/\eta}-1.
\]

**Executable post-processor**:
```python
@jit
```
