### 1. Complete mathematical apparatus (paper equations, definitions, theorems)

**Wiener amalgam spaces.**  
For \(1\le r,q\le\infty\) the space \(E^r_q(\mathbb{R}^3)\) is defined by the norm
\[
\|f\|_{E^r_q}:=\Bigl\|\|f\|_{L^r(B_1(k))}\Bigr\|_{\ell^q(k\in\mathbb{Z}^3)},
\]
where \(B_1(k)=k+[0,1)^3\). When \(q=\infty\) one recovers the uniformly local space \(L^r_{\rm uloc}\). The spaces interpolate between classical Lebesgue spaces (\(q=r\)) and purely local spaces. Hölder product estimates hold:
\[
\|fg\|_{E^r_q}\le\|f\|_{E^{r_1}_{q_1}}\|g\|_{E^{r_2}_{q_2}}
\]
whenever \(1/r=1/r_1+1/r_2\) and \(1/q\le1/q_1+1/q_2\). Spacetime versions are
\[
\|f\|_{L^s_TE^r_q}=\|f\|_{L^s(0,T;E^r_q)},\qquad
\|f\|_{E^{s,p}_{T,q}}=\Bigl\|\|f\|_{L^s(0,T;L^p(B_1(k)))}\Bigr\|_{\ell^q}.
\]

**Incompressible MHD system (MHD).**  
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tv-\Delta v+(v\cdot\nabla)v-(b\cdot\nabla)b+\nabla\pi&=0,\\
\partial_tb-\Delta b+(v\cdot\nabla)b-(b\cdot\nabla)v&=0,\\
\nabla\cdot v=\nabla\cdot b&=0,
\end{aligned}
\]
with divergence-free initial data \((v_0,b_0)\). (The magnetic stretching and advection appear with opposite signs relative to some engineering formulations; the paper’s form is the mathematically standard ideal-MHD reduction after the Lorentz force is rewritten via \(\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}\).)

**Viscoelastic Navier–Stokes with damping (vNSEd).**  
Let \(F=(f_1,f_2,f_3)\) be the deformation tensor (columns \(f_m\)). After setting the damping coefficient \(\mu=1\) and imposing \(\nabla\cdot F=0\), the system reads
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tv-\Delta v+(v\cdot\nabla)v-\sum_{n=1}^3(f_n\cdot\nabla)f_n+\nabla\pi&=0,\\
\partial_tf_m-\Delta f_m+(v\cdot\nabla)f_m-(f_m\cdot\nabla)v&=0,\qquad m=1,2,3,\\
\nabla\cdot v=\nabla\cdot f_m&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The structure is formally identical to MHD upon the identification \(b\leftrightarrow(f_1,f_2,f_3)\).

**Mild formulation.**  
Let \(\mathrm{e}^{t\Delta}\) be the heat semigroup and \(\mathbb{P}\) the Helmholtz–Leray projector. A mild solution of MHD satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
v(t)&=\mathrm{e}^{t\Delta}v_0-B_1((v,b),(v,b))(t),\\
b(t)&=\mathrm{e}^{t\Delta}b_0-B_2((v,b),(v,b))(t),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the bilinear operators are
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_1((v,b),(u,a))(t)&=\int_0^t\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v\otimes u-b\otimes a)\,ds,\\
B_2((v,b),(u,a))(t)&=\int_0^t\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v\otimes a-b\otimes u)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
Component-wise they involve the Oseen tensor \(S_{ij}\). The identical structure holds for vNSEd with the sum over the three deformation columns replacing the magnetic quadratic terms.

**Main mild-solution theorems (critical / subcritical).**  
- **Subcritical** (\(r>3\), any \(q\in[1,\infty]\)): for data of size \(\varepsilon\) there exists \(T\simeq\varepsilon^{-2/(1-3/r)}\) such that a unique mild solution lives in
  \[
  L^\infty(0,T;E^r_q\times E^r_q)\cap C((0,T);E^r_q\times E^r_q)
  \]
  and obeys the spacetime bounds
  \[
  \|(v,b)\|_{E^{s,p}_{T,m}}\lesssim\|(v_0,b_0)\|_{E^r_q}
  \]
  for all admissible pairs \((s,p)\) with \(2/s+3/p=3/r\).
- **Critical** (\(r=3\)): for small data in \(E^3_q\) one obtains global (or large-\(T\)) solutions in the Kato-type spaces
  \[
  \mathcal{E}_T=\bigl\{(v,b):\sup_t\|(v,b)(t)\|_{E^3_q}+t^{1/2}\|(v,b)(t)\|_{E^\infty_{q_2}}<\infty\bigr\}
  \]
  and the auxiliary space \(\mathcal{F}_T\) with weight \(t^{1/4}\) in \(E^6_{q_1}\). The bilinear map \(B\) is a contraction on a ball of \(\mathcal{F}_T\) of radius proportional to the data size.

**Local-energy weak solutions.**  
A pair \((v,b)\) is a local-energy solution if it belongs to the amalgam energy space
\[
\mathbf{LE}_q(0,T)=\bigl\{(v,b):\text{ess-sup}_t\|(v,b)(t)\|_{E^2_q}^2+\|\nabla(v,b)\|_{L^2_TE^2_q}^2<\infty\bigr\}
\]
and satisfies the local energy inequality on every cylinder \(Q_r(x_0,t_0)\), the pressure representation via the Riesz kernel, and strong continuity of the local \(L^2\) norms as \(t\to0^+\). Theorems 1.8–1.14 establish:
- global existence for arbitrary data in \(E^2_q\) (\(q\ge1\));
- initial regularity (smoothness for \(t>0\) small when the high-frequency part of the data is small in \(N_R^0\));
- eventual regularity (smoothness for large \(t\) under a uniform local-energy smallness condition);
- small-large uniqueness: a local-energy solution that is small in a high-frequency amalgam norm coincides with any other local-energy solution sharing the same data.

All proofs proceed by first constructing mild solutions via fixed-point arguments that exploit the decay estimates of the Stokes semigroup in amalgam spaces (identical to those proved for pure Navier–Stokes by Bradshaw–Lai–Tsai), then passing to the limit in a regularised / localised Galerkin approximation while preserving the local energy inequality, and finally applying a perturbation argument that separates a small critical piece from a large energy piece.
