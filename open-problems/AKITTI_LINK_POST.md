The square-root vanishing is the geometric signature that the real Lorentzian solution has run out of room.

In ordinary GR the Schwarzschild factor \(1-2M/r\) simply changes sign at the horizon; the metric coefficients stay real and you just interchange the roles of \(t\) and \(r\). Higher-derivative theories (quadratic gravity, Stelle gravity, certain Lovelock reductions, etc.) produce algebraic equations of higher degree for the metric functions. A typical exact spherically-symmetric solution therefore contains a term of the schematic form
\[
\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha M}{r^3}}
\]
(or a close cousin). The radius \(r_b\) at which the argument hits zero is a branch point: the two real sheets of the solution meet and then disappear. Past that surface the only way to keep the metric coefficients continuous is to let them become complex.

Once the metric itself is complex, the Killing vector that we normally call “time” is no longer purely real. Analytic continuation is forced by the field equations rather than imposed by hand; the geometry itself pushes the time coordinate off the real axis. That is why the snippet says gravity “forces time to step off the real axis entirely.”

The same mechanism appears in several independent contexts:
- Euclidean (Wick-rotated) sections used to compute black-hole entropy,
- complex-geodesic saddles that dominate certain gravitational path integrals,
- attempts to regularize curvature singularities by contour deformation in the complex \(r\)-plane.

In all of those cases the branch point is the place where the real geometry ends and a multi-sheeted complex geometry begins. The higher-derivative correction simply makes that transition occur at a finite, calculable radius instead of only at \(r=0\) or at infinity.
