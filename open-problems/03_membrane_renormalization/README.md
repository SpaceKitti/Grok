# Open problem 3: renormalization of membranes and higher-dimensional world-volumes

## Problem
Strings are the only extended objects with a power-counting renormalizable world-sheet quantization; for $p$-branes with $p \ge 2$ the world-volume theory is non-renormalizable and generates an infinite tower of counterterms. The quantized (super)membrane, regularized as $SU(N)$ matrix quantum mechanics, has a continuous spectrum (flat directions), so it does not describe a discrete particle-like spectrum the way the string does. No power-counting renormalizable quantization of membranes or higher world-volumes is known; the matrix-model reinterpretation (BFSS) reframes rather than resolves the problem.

## Papers (open, arXiv-verified)
1. M. J. Duff, *Supermembranes*, TASI 1996 lectures (1996). https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9611203 , file `hep-th_9611203_Duff_supermembranes_TASI.pdf`. Standard review of the supermembrane action, its coupling to $D=11$ supergravity, and why membranes were long dismissed (non-renormalizability, no obvious perturbative expansion).
2. H. Nicolai, R. Helling, *Supermembranes and M(atrix) Theory* (1998). https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9809103 , file `hep-th_9809103_Nicolai_supermembranes_matrix_theory.pdf`. Lectures deriving the matrix regularization of the membrane and explaining the continuous spectrum, plus how it is reinterpreted as multi-particle states in M-theory.
3. T. Banks, W. Fischler, S. H. Shenker, L. Susskind, *M Theory As A Matrix Model: A Conjecture* (1996). https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9610043 , file `hep-th_9610043_Banks_BFSS_matrix_model.pdf`. Landmark BFSS paper: the same matrix quantum mechanics, taken at large $N$, is proposed as a non-perturbative definition of M-theory, turning the "bug" of the continuous spectrum into a feature.
4. W. Taylor, *M(atrix) Theory: Matrix Quantum Mechanics as a Fundamental Theory* (2001). https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0101126 , file `hep-th_0101126_Taylor_matrix_theory_review.pdf`. Review (Rev. Mod. Phys.) of matrix theory: membrane regularization, interactions, loop computations, and what remains open.
