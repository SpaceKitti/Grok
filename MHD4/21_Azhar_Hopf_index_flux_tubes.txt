### 1. Emergent magnetic field and flux-tube decomposition (paper §2)
The normalised magnetisation \(\mathbf{m}(\mathbf{r})\) (\(|\mathbf{m}|=1\)) defines the emergent magnetic field
\[
F^k = \frac{1}{8\pi}\epsilon^{ijk}\,\mathbf{m}\cdot(\partial_i\mathbf{m}\times\partial_j\mathbf{m}).
\]
\(\mathbf{F}\) is divergence-free by construction (\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}=0\)). Its field lines are organised into \(N_\Phi\) flux tubes of fluxes
\[
\Phi_i=\Bigl|\iint_{S_i}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{a}\Bigr|,
\]
where \(S_i\) is any cross-section of tube \(i\). For a ferromagnetic background \(m_z^{\mathrm{b}}=\pm1\) one has \(\Phi_i\in\mathbb{Z}\); for a spiral background \(m_z^{\mathrm{b}}=0\) the tubes are meronic with \(\Phi_i\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2\). In the general mixed case \(0<|m_z^{\mathrm{b}}|<1\) the two hemispheres of \(S^2\) (split by the latitude \(\theta=\arcsin m_z^{\mathrm{b}}\)) carry continuous fluxes
\[
\Phi_\pm=\frac{1\pm m_z^{\mathrm{b}}}{2}.
\]
The discrete geometric Hopf index is the flux-weighted sum of linking numbers:
\[
H=\sum_{i=1}^{N_\Phi}L_{ii}\Phi_i^2+2\sum_{1\le i<j\le N_\Phi}L_{ij}\Phi_i\Phi_j.
\]
(The Whitehead integral \(H=\int\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{A}\,d^3r\) is recovered in the continuum limit but is gauge-dependent and numerically fragile; the discrete form is model-independent and exact for periodic textures.)

The linking numbers themselves are given by the Gauss double integral along oriented field-line curves \(C_i,C_j\):
\[
L_{ij}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{C_i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{(\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}_j)\cdot(d\mathbf{r}_i\times d\mathbf{r}_j)}{|\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}_j|^3}.
\]
Self-linking \(L_{ii}\) is evaluated by a limiting procedure (or by the Călugăreanu–White–Fuller formula \(L_{ii}=\mathrm{Tw}+\mathrm{Wr}\)). Linking numbers are topological invariants under continuous deformations that preserve the background latitude; only the fluxes \(\Phi_i\) (and therefore \(H\)) respond to changes in \(m_z^{\mathrm{b}}\).

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import dblquad

def gauss_linking(C1, C2, n_samples=200):
    """Discrete Gauss linking number for two closed curves."""
    t = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, n_samples, endpoint=False)
    r1 = np.array([C1(ti) for ti in t])
    r2 = np.array([C2(ti) for ti in t])
    dr1 = np.gradient(r1, axis=0)
    dr2 = np.gradient(r2, axis=0)
    L = 0.0
    for i in range(n_samples):
        for j in range(n_samples):
            diff = r1[i] - r2[j]
            cross = np.cross(dr1[i], dr2[j])
            L += np.dot(diff, cross) / (np.linalg.norm(diff)**3 + 1e-12)
    return -L / (4*np.pi * n_samples**2)

def hopf_index(fluxes, self_links, inter_links):
    """Discrete geometric Hopf index."""
    H = sum(Lii * Phi**2 for Lii, Phi in zip(self_links, fluxes))
    for i in range(len(fluxes)):
        for j in range(i+1, len(fluxes)):
            H += 2 * inter_links[(i,j)] * fluxes[i] * fluxes[j]
    return H
```
