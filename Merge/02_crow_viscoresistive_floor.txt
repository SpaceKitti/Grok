This is a specialized decaying-MHD / current-sheet question. I’ll look up the relevant dissipation literature (including Kuzzay 2019) and typical energy-drop floors at comparable viscosity/resistivity before answering.The 6% is the honest viscoresistive floor of this run, not a leftover leak. Most of it is already present in pure hydro.  ## What the books are actually saying  In a periodic incompressible box the only global sinks are the dissipative ones:  \[ \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(E_u+E_B\bigr) =-\nu\langle|\omega|^2\rangle-\eta\langle|J|^2\rangle \quad\text{(plus whatever hyperviscous/hyperresistive work you added).} \]  Lorentz work is not a third sink. Globally  \[ W_L=\langle u\cdot(J\times B)\rangle=-\langle B_i S_{ij}B_j\rangle, \]  so it only moves energy between \(E_u\) and \(E_B\). Locally the two expressions differ by a divergence: Maxwell-stress / Poynting flux through the surface of a sheet control volume. That is the Kuzzay 2019 leftover — spatial flux through the sheet edge, not extra heat. Turning the hive scars off and watching the kinetic books close is exactly the check that this identity is intact.  Your own earlier Crow + 4-scar table already showed the same number: NS alone \(\Delta E_\text{tot}\approx 5.95\%\) at \(t=0.36\); NS+MHD with \(b_\text{guide}=z\), \(\eta=10^{-3}\) was \(6.26\%\). After scars off the drop stayed \(\sim 6\%\). So almost all of the six percent is ordinary viscous burn of the Crow pair. MHD added a few tenths of a percent of Ohmic and stored some of the missing kinetic energy as \(E_B\).  At this viscosity and this resistivity, a few-percent total drop by \(t=0.36\) is therefore the expected floor of the equations you actually integrated. It is not a bug and it is not “the stretching.”  ## Is that the ideal-plasma floor?  No. It is the floor of **this** \((\nu,\eta)\) and **this** grid. A nearly-ideal plasma is the limit \(\nu,\eta\to 0\) with the sheets still seen.  Two different limits get mixed in the literature:  1. **Short-time coherent Crow evolution** (your toy). Most dissipation sits in the large-scale strain of the tubes, not in a developed cascade. NS already loses \(\sim 6\%\) by \(t=0.36\). Lowering \(\nu\) at fixed resolved strain should drop that number roughly like the viscous work \(\int\nu\langle\omega^2\rangle\,dt\). Resistivity mainly decides how much of the stolen kinetic energy is stored in \(B\) versus burned as \(\eta J^2\) inside the sheets. 2. **Developed decaying MHD turbulence.** There the dissipation rate at the peak of \(\varepsilon(t)\) asymptotes to a finite value as \(\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Rm}\to\infty\) (dissipative anomaly; Mininni–Pouquet, Linkmann et al.). That is reconnection / current-sheet thinning keeping \(\eta\langle J^2\rangle\) from vanishing. You are not obviously in that regime at \(t=0.36\) on an \(N=48\) Crow pair.  So: a few percent is the expected floor **at the coefficients you used**. It is not the expected floor of a nearly-ideal plasma.  ## What actually lets the number fall  In order of leverage for this specific toy:  **Higher Reynolds (lower \(\nu\)).** This is the main knob. The 6% is mostly hydro friction of the Crow tubes. Raise \(\mathrm{Re}\) until the integrated viscous work at \(t=0.36\) is the small number you want. Keep the pair resolved; otherwise you just replace \(\nu\) with grid viscosity and the books will lie again.  **Better-resolved sheets, not a different stretching story.** Sheet thickness in this setup is set by a competition between stretching and \(\eta\) (Sweet–Parker-like \(\delta\sim S^{-1/2}\) until tearing). If the grid does not put several points across \(\delta\), two bad things happen: high-\(k\)-only hyperresistivity misses the sheet (you already saw that), and all-mode hyperresistivity dulls \(\max|J|\) while barely touching \(W_L\). Ohmic then becomes a numerical accident. Resolve \(\delta\), then \(\eta\langle J^2\rangle\) is the physical burn and \(W_L\) is allowed to store rather than dump.  **A tension-safe regulariser that still sees the sheet.** Requirements, from the energy identity:  - no new global sink (the scars-off test you already ran); - must not mollify the velocity that enters the induction equation (that was the earlier fake diffusivity); - must leave the Maxwell stress inside the sheet intact, so \(W_L\) can still store; - dissipation, if any, should sit on \(J\) at the sheet scale, not as a broadband drain on every mode.  All-mode hyperresistivity fails the third/fourth points (dulls the spike, little effect on theft). High-\(k\)-only failed to see the sheet at this \(N\). A usable option is explicit resistivity at the physical \(\eta\) you want, plus enough points across the sheet, or a current-threshold / anomalous \(\eta(J)\) that fires only where \(|J|\) is actually large. Do not invent a new leak to hide the 6%.  **Higher Lundquist at fixed resolved sheet.** That shifts the KE loss from Ohmic burn toward magnetic storage. Total \(E_u+E_B\) still drops only by \(\int(\nu\omega^2+\eta J^2)\,dt\); you just change the partition. Lower \(\eta\) without resolving the thinner sheet will not give you the ideal limit — it gives you an under-resolved spike and a different numerical \(\eta\).  ## Do you need new papers for the floor?  Not for stretching, and not for the identity. Those are closed.  What is **not** sitting on a shelf as “Crow tubes, spectral MHD, \(\Delta E(t=0.36)\) vs \(\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Rm}\)” is your exact initial condition. The papers that exist are:  - local sheet flux vs volume conversion (Kuzzay 2019; Aluie-type coarse-graining); - dissipative anomaly in decaying MHD at the **peak** of \(\varepsilon\), not at a short coherent time; - reconnection-controlled late decay (Hosking–Schekochihin); - antiparallel vortex tubes with frozen-in flux tubes and Lorentz work during joint reconnection (the 2025 vortex–magnetic competition DNS).  None of those replace an \(\mathrm{Re}\)–\(\mathrm{Rm}\) scan of **this** decaying Crow pair with scars off and a closed energy budget. That scan is the measurement of the floor. If total drop at \(t=0.36\) falls with \(\nu\) while \(E_u+E_B+\int(\nu\omega^2+\eta J^2)\,dt\) stays conserved, you are done. If it plateaus while sheets tear and fill the box, you have hit the anomaly and further lowering \(\nu,\eta\) will not help without a different regulariser or a different question.  Practical hive test, no new theory: same Crow IC, scars off, three viscosities (current, \(\times 1/2\), \(\times 1/4\)) at fixed well-resolved \(\eta\), then one run with \(\eta\) dropped only after the sheet is measured to be \(\ge 4\)–\(6\) points thick. The number that moves is the floor. The number that does not move was never heat.
