## Core Object: The Seam
A seam is the locus where two regimes meet and neither set of rules fully owns the territory.
It is not a boundary in the classical sense (which belongs to one side) and not a bulk (which is owned by one description). It is the transition zone that exists only while the handoff is live.

Canonical realisations:
- Graphene electrode contact (lattice-ending / lead-beginning)
- Soft-robotic viscoelastic body–fluid interface
- Zero-eigenvalue soft modes of a Hessian (flat directions that have not yet chosen which way to bend)
- Open-to-closed flux surface transition in MHD
- Pre-/post-Page-time information channel switch

## Governing Sequence
1. Scattering (driver) — the two regimes collide; the old rules cease to apply.
2. Intermediate patch — a third, temporary regime that belongs to neither side. Rules are renegotiated in real time.
3. Cubic form (collapse) — the system folds into a definite new configuration. The cubic does not drive the transition; it is what the system collapses into once the intermediate patch can no longer hold open.

The intermediate patch is the seam at its widest. Residual structures:
- Oldroyd-B residual channels (stress history that is neither solid nor fluid)
- Prandtl boundary layer (neither free-stream nor wall)
- Boundary residual modes of an entanglement Hamiltonian (soft eps that freeze as M->inf, leakage O(M^-2))
- Folded flux as the intermediate state between open and closed field lines

## Topological Decision Rule (Open-Ridge vs Closed-Tube)
Geometry alone does not decide the outcome; topology of the boundary does.
- Open buckle / open ridge breaks inversion symmetry -> net dipole / net helicity injection.
- Closed tube / closed flux surface cancels opposing contributions -> zero net charge or cancelled helicity.
Same local curvature, opposite global result, selected by whether the defect is topologically open or closed.
Maps onto: graphene flexoelectric polarisation (Rice et al. sub-nm curvature); MHD flux-rope helicity injection and interchange reconnection; firewall complementarity (topology selects which patch is available).

## Lag Structure
The cubic form appears slightly downstream of where eigenvalues kiss / curvature peaks.
- Nozzle contour: stress peak downstream of curvature peak
- Graphene: net dipole peaks slightly downstream of maximum curvature
- MHD: helicity accumulates just downstream along the rope before reconnection
- Soft actuator: peak force does not land at maximum strain
Two kinds of lag: temporal (cubic offset in time) and spatial (boundary-residual offset of the entanglement Hamiltonian). The offset inherits the dimension in which the seam was born; the seam is coordinate-agnostic.

## Bifurcation Language (Brockett / Normal-Form)
When two negative eigenvalues approach and kiss, cubic forms emerge at the merge. Organising normal forms: pitchfork, fold, cusp. Unequal winding or Lorentz feedback unfolds the pitchfork into a fold; topology actively selects the splitting mode. Zero eigenvalues are the Hessian signature of the intermediate patch.

## Information / Page-Time Mapping
- Before Page time: Bell-basis / EPR-like (teleportation) channel; decoding needs a large early-radiation buffer.
- After Page time: local-basis (mode annihilation) channel; single-qudit decoding saturates to fidelity 1.
Scattering = fast local annihilation; cubic = slow downstream reorganisation on the Page-time scale. ER=EPR as the geometric statement of the seam; open-ridge/closed-tube as topological complementarity.

## Working Principles
1. The seam is where the rules are renegotiated; it is not owned by either side.
2. Scattering opens the intermediate patch; the cubic form closes it.
3. Topology (open vs closed) decides the sign and the available description.
4. The cubic arrives with a small but systematic lag; the lag inherits the dimension of the seam.
5. Zero modes, residual channels, and folded flux are different clothes on the same intermediate patch.
6. The same sequence appears in materials, fluids, MHD, and QI because the seam is coordinate- and substrate-agnostic.
