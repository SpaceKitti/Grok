**Unified Compilation of Set 1: E-brane Clay / C*Hive Framework Stack**  
(Kinematic Stretching Suppression + Vortex Filament Cascades/Reconnection + Pressure-Quotient Velocity Clocks + Large-Data Stress-Diffusive Rate-Type Existence + 2-D Diffusive Strong Regularity)

This set forms a tightly convergent backbone for the 3-D incompressible Navier–Stokes + rate-type viscoelastic (Oldroyd-B / Giesekus / Johnson–Segalman) system that defines the E-brane clay / C*Hive. All five strips target the same core obstacles: control of the vortex-stretching term \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}\) (and therefore enstrophy production and \(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_\infty\)), quantitative polymer-work diagnostics \(\boldsymbol{\tau}:\mathbf{S}\), preservation of positive-definiteness of the conformation/extra-stress, energy structure that isolates genuine elastic dissipation from excess numerical drain, and practical continuation / blow-up monitors that can be inserted into adaptive spectral or residual-based solvers.  

Convergence is strong on the algebraic–energetic–diagnostic level and partial (but actionable) on the pure existence/regularity level. Taken together they produce a two-parameter regularisation manifold \((\lambda,\kappa)\) (or \((\lambda,\eta_p)\)) that can be dialled while the full conformation equation is retained for energy-balance fidelity, exactly the optimisation surface needed to drive excess dissipation toward the solvent floor while retaining (or enhancing) regularising effects on reconnection and peak enstrophy.

### Individual Notes (Set 1)

**1. Bos–Shao–Wu–Fang (arXiv:2508.15349) – Kinematic \(\lambda\)-suppression of vortex stretching**  
Pure hydrodynamic one-parameter family that multiplies the stretching term by \((1-\lambda)\) while restoring \(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}=0\) via a Lagrange multiplier. Yields exact analytic wall-unit profiles
\[
U^+(y^+)=\frac1\kappa\frac{(y^+)^{\Gamma}-1}{\Gamma}+U_0^+(\lambda),\qquad\Gamma=\frac\lambda{2-\lambda}
\]
and a closed friction-factor expression that collapses polymer drag-reduction data under the linear map \(\lambda=(Wi-0.5)/10\). Enstrophy production scales exactly with \((1-\lambda)\). Drop-in replacement for the vorticity update; hybridises immediately with any Oldroyd-B stress evolution. Limitation: phenomenological mixing-length closure; free-space singularity formation not addressed. Directly quantifies how much of observed excess dissipation is “stretching attenuation” versus genuine \(\boldsymbol{\tau}:\mathbf{S}\).

**2. Ostilla-Mónico–McKeown–Brenner–Rubinstein–Pumir (arXiv:2102.11133) – Cascades & reconnection in angled vortex filaments**  
Controlled DNS of two counter-rotating Gaussian tubes at angle \(\beta=2\arctan(1/b)\). Transition loci at \(\operatorname{Re}_\Gamma\approx4000\): Crow/sheet regime \(\beta\gtrsim67.4^\circ\), transitional, elliptical cascade \(\beta\lesssim53.1^\circ\). Peak dissipation essentially independent of \(\operatorname{Re}_\Gamma\) once sheets form; sixth-moment \(M_6=\langle|\boldsymbol{\omega}|^6\rangle^{1/3}\) grows strongly. Exact initial data, symmetry, and diagnostics (\(E\), \(\varepsilon=\nu\langle|\boldsymbol{\omega}|^2\rangle\), \(M_{2n}\), band-pass topology) become the Newtonian baseline. Limitation: pure NS, moderate \(\operatorname{Re}_\Gamma\). Essential unit-test geometry for every subsequent viscoelastic claim about reconnection delay or spatial delocalisation of the fine-scale cloud.

**3. Sai Peng (arXiv:2606.25258v4) – Pressure quotients & endpoint velocity clocks (non-diffusive)**  
Spectral-isotropic stress decomposes into isotropic pressure + active-deviatoric channel \(\tau(C)^-=q_1(a,|Y|^2)Y\) (2-D exact; 3-D residual \(q_2(Y^2)^\circ\) vanishes for pure Oldroyd-B). Weighted active energy
\[
E_{\rm act}=\int\Bigl(\tfrac12|u|^2+\tfrac{q_1}{2a}|Y|^2+\sigma|h|^2\Bigr)\,dx
\]
cancels the leading stretching–divergence coupling under the weight \(q_1/a\). Continuation criteria become pure velocity clocks: \(\nabla u\in L^1_t B^0_{\infty,1}\) (Oldroyd-B 2-D) or \(L^2_t B^0_{\infty,1}\) (FENE-P). The clock forces the conformation into a compact spectral window. Limitation: exact scalar closure is 2-D; 3-D residual controlled only on a-priori compact windows. Supplies the algebraic structure that lets any residual-based or spectral solver monitor the only channel that can stretch vorticity.

**4. Bathory–Bulíček–Málek (arXiv:2002.11224) – Large-data 3-D existence with stress diffusion**  
Global weak solutions for arbitrarily large data on any \([0,T]\) for a convex combination of diffusive Oldroyd-B and Giesekus with arbitrary Johnson–Segalman parameter \(a\). Thermodynamically consistent free energy
\[
\psi(\mathbb{B})=\mu\Bigl[(1-\gamma)(\operatorname{tr}\mathbb{B}-3-\ln\det\mathbb{B})+\tfrac\gamma2|\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{I}|^2\Bigr]
\]
forces \(\mathbb{B}\) into the same spaces as classical NS velocity and yields the exact energy inequality containing viscous, polymer and stress-diffusion dissipation. Positive-definiteness is preserved by the approximation scheme. Limitation: weak solutions only; no uniqueness or quantitative \(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_\infty\) control. The free-energy + dissipation structure is the natural continuum “clay” constitutive law.

**5. 2-D Oldroyd-B with stress diffusion (arXiv:1204.3503)**  
Global strong solutions (and higher-Sobolev propagation) for large data in 2-D once \(\kappa\Delta\sigma\) is present. Positivity preserved by a maximum-principle argument on \(\gamma=c-2\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). Energy identity isolates the polymer-work term; diffusion supplies the \(L^2\) control on \(\nabla\sigma\) that closes all higher estimates. Limitation: strictly 2-D (no vortex stretching); constants explode as \(\kappa\to0\). Cleanest quantitative illustration of the regularising power of even a small stress diffusivity.

### Convergence Map & Unified Mathematical Structure

All five strips converge on the same projected system
\[
\begin{align*}
\partial_t\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}&=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=0,\\
\mathbb{B}^\diamond+\delta_1(\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{I})+\delta_2(\mathbb{B}^2-\mathbb{B})&=\lambda\Delta\mathbb{B}
\quad\text{(or the pure Oldroyd-B limit)},
\end{align*}
\]
where \(\boldsymbol{\tau}=2\mu a\bigl[(1-\gamma)(\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{I})+\gamma(\mathbb{B}^2-\mathbb{B})\bigr]\) (or the simpler \(\mu(\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{I})\)) and \(\mathbb{B}^\diamond\) is any objective derivative.  

- **Kinematic dial** inserts the factor \((1-\lambda)\) in front of the stretching term that appears after taking the curl (or, equivalently, multiplies the nonlinear term in the primitive-variable form).  
- **Filament initial data** specialise the free-space Cauchy problem to the two-tube geometry that realises the Crow/elliptical cascade transition.  
- **Pressure-quotient projection** rewrites \(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau}\) as the active channel \(q_1 Y\) (plus residual that vanishes for Oldroyd-B), allowing the weighted energy \(E_{\rm act}\) to cancel the leading coupling.  
- **Free-energy structure** of the large-data theory supplies the exact energy inequality whose residual is the numerical excess dissipation that must be minimised.  
- **Stress diffusion** \(\kappa\Delta\mathbb{B}\) (or \(\kappa\Delta\sigma\)) is the minimal regularisation that upgrades the 2-D theory to global strong solutions and the 3-D theory to global weak solutions; it is compatible with every other piece.

The resulting hybrid enstrophy budget reads
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_2^2=(1-\lambda)\langle\omega_i\omega_j S_{ij}\rangle+\text{polymer-work contributions from }\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\tau})-\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_2^2.
\]
A weighted BKM-type monitor becomes
\[
\int_0^T\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_\infty}{1+c|\mathcal{W}|+\delta}\,dt,\qquad\mathcal{W}=\int\boldsymbol{\tau}:\mathbf{S}\,dV,
\]
while the pressure-quotient clock \(\|\nabla u\|_{B^0_{\infty,1}}\) and the free-energy \(\psi(\mathbb{B})\) remain under simultaneous surveillance. Positive-definiteness is inherited from both the maximum-principle argument (2-D) and the approximation scheme of the large-data theory (3-D).

**Notes on residual gaps.** Exact scalar cancellation is 2-D; the 3-D residual is absorbed by viscosity only on compact windows (or by diffusion). The kinematic \(\lambda\) is not self-consistent with a conformation equation; it must be retained as an independent dial or calibrated against \(\eta_p\). Weak solutions from the large-data theory do not yet yield quantitative reconnection-delay theorems; those remain numerical targets on the filament geometry.

```python
import numpy as np
from itertools import product

# ---------- 1. Filament initial data (Ostilla-Mónico et al.) ----------
def angled_vortex_pair(Nx, Ny, Nz, Lx=2*np.pi, Ly=2*np.pi, b=1.0,
                       sigma=0.35, d=0.9, Gamma=1.0):
    x = np.linspace(0, Lx, Nx, endpoint=False)
    y = np.linspace(0, Ly, Ny, endpoint=False)
    Lz = 2 * b * np.pi
    z = np.linspace(0, Lz, Nz, endpoint=False)
    X, Y, Z = np.meshgrid(x, y, z, indexing='ij')
    e_p = np.array([1., 0.,  b]) / np.sqrt(1 + b*b)
    e_m = np.array([1., 0., -b]) / np.sqrt(1 + b*b)
    def dist(X, Y, Z, e, y0):
        rx, ry, rz = X, Y - y0, Z
        proj = rx*e[0] + ry*e[1] + rz*e[2]
        return np.sqrt((rx-proj*e[0])**2 + (ry-proj*e[1])**2 + (rz-proj*e[2])**2)
    rho_p = dist(X, Y, Z, e_p, +d/2)
    rho_m = dist(X, Y, Z, e_m, -d/2)
    Omega = Gamma / (sigma**2)
    amp_p =  Omega * np.exp(-rho_p**2 / (2*sigma**2))
    amp_m = -Omega * np.exp(-rho_m**2 / (2*sigma**2))
    wx = amp_p*e_p[0] + amp_m*e_m[0]
    wy = amp_p*e_p[1] + amp_m*e_m[1]
    wz = amp_p*e_p[2] + amp_m*e_m[2]
    return np.stack([wx, wy, wz], axis=0)

# ---------- 2. Kinematic stretching factor (Bos et al.) ----------
def enstrophy_production(omega, S, lam):
    prod = 0.0
    for i, j in product(range(3), repeat=2):
        prod += omega[i] * omega[j] * S[i, j]
    return (1.0 - lam) * np.sum(prod)

# ---------- 3. Pressure-quotient active energy (Sai Peng) ----------
def active_energy(u, C, q1_func, a0=1.0, sigma=1.0):
    a = 0.5 * np.trace(C, axis1=-2, axis2=-1)
    Y = C - a[..., None, None] * np.eye(3)
    q1 = q1_func(a, np.sum(Y**2, axis=(-2, -1)))
    Eu = 0.5 * np.sum(u**2)
    EY = 0.5 * np.sum((q1 / (a + 1e-12)) * np.sum(Y**2, axis=(-2, -1)))
    Eh = 0.5 * sigma * np.sum((a - a0)**2)
    return Eu + EY + Eh

# ---------- 4. Free-energy & mixed stress (Bathory–Bulíček–Málek) ----------
def free_energy(B, gamma, mu):
    trB = np.trace(B, axis1=-2, axis2=-1)
    detB = np.linalg.det(B)
    log_term = trB - 3 - np.log(detB + 1e-30)
    quad = 0.5 * np.sum((B - np.eye(3))**2, axis=(-2, -1))
    return mu * ((1 - gamma) * log_term + gamma * quad)

def extra_stress(B, gamma, mu, a):
    I = np.eye(3)
    S = (1 - gamma) * (B - I) + gamma * (B @ B - B)
    return 2 * mu * a * S

# ---------- 5. Objective derivative + optional stress diffusion ----------
def objective_derivative(B, grad_v, a, material_deriv):
    Dv = 0.5 * (grad_v + np.swapaxes(grad_v, -2, -1))
    Wv = 0.5 * (grad_v - np.swapaxes(grad_v, -2, -1))
    return (material_deriv
            - a * (Dv @ B + B @ Dv)
            - (Wv @ B - B @ Wv))

def stress_diffusion_term(B, kappa, lap_B):
    return kappa * lap_B

# ---------- 6. Hybrid residual skeleton (matrix-free ready) ----------
def hybrid_residual(U, grad_U, B, lam, gamma, mu, a_param, kappa,
                    beta_solvent=0.5, Wi=1.0, eta0=1.0):
    """
    U = [u (3,), p, chi or B]
    Returns residual of momentum + continuity + constitutive
    with kinematic (1-lam) factor available for diagnostics.
    """
    u, p, chi = U  # or B
    eps = 0.5 * (grad_U[0] + np.swapaxes(grad_U[0], -1, -2))
    Omega = 0.5 * (grad_U[0] - np.swapaxes(grad_U[0], -1, -2))
    # Saramito / log-conf maps can be inserted here if desired
    tau = extra_stress(B, gamma, mu, a_param)
    R_mom = (u · ∇)u - 2*beta_solvent*eta0*div(eps) - div(tau) + ∇p
    R_con = div(u)
    # constitutive with optional diffusion and objective derivative
    R_const = objective_derivative(B, grad_U[0], a_param, material_term) \
              + relaxation_terms(B) - stress_diffusion_term(B, kappa, lap_B)
    # optional ASGS stabilisation parameters can be multiplied here
    return pack(R_mom, R_con, R_const)

# ---------- 7. Live monitors (all five papers) ----------
def weighted_BKM(omega_inf, work, c=0.25, delta=1e-8):
    return omega_inf / (1.0 + c * abs(work) + delta)

def simultaneous_diagnostics(u, omega, tau, B, lam, gamma, mu):
    S = 0.5 * (grad(u) + grad(u).T)
    ens_prod = enstrophy_production(omega, S, lam)
    work = np.sum(tau * S)
    E_act = active_energy(u, B, lambda a, Y2: 1.0)  # placeholder q1
    psi = free_energy(B, gamma, mu)
    M6 = np.mean(np.abs(omega)**6)**(1/3)
    return {
        'enstrophy_prod': ens_prod,
        'polymer_work': work,
        'active_energy': E_act,
        'free_energy': psi,
        'M6': M6,
        'max_omega': np.max(np.abs(omega))
    }
```

### (i) Unified residual with kinematic dial, stress diffusion and free-energy structure

The three-field ASGS residual on adaptive tetrahedra is extended by the Set-1 constitutive law:

\[
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{R}_{\rm mom}&=\rho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\cdot\bigl(2\beta\eta_0\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}+\boldsymbol{\tau}\bigr)+\nabla p,\\
\mathbf{R}_{\rm con}&=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},\\
\mathbf{R}_{\rm const}&=\mathbb{B}^\diamond+\delta_1(\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{I})+\delta_2(\mathbb{B}^2-\mathbb{B})-\kappa\Delta\mathbb{B},
\end{align*}
\]

where \(\boldsymbol{\tau}=2\mu a\bigl[(1-\gamma)(\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{I})+\gamma(\mathbb{B}^2-\mathbb{B})\bigr]\) (or the pure Oldroyd-B limit) and the objective derivative \(\mathbb{B}^\diamond\) is evaluated with arbitrary \(a\). The kinematic factor \(\lambda\) appears only in the post-processed enstrophy production

\[
\langle\omega_i\omega_j S_{ij}\rangle_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)\langle\omega_i\omega_j S_{ij}\rangle
\]

so that the residual itself remains variationally consistent with the full viscoelastic stress. Stabilisation parameters \(\alpha_{\rm mom},\alpha_{\rm con},\alpha_{\rm const}\) continue to be computed element-wise from local mesh size, velocity magnitude and polymer time scale; the same residual estimator \(\eta_T\) drives newest-vertex bisection.

```python
import torch
device = torch.device('cuda' if http://torch.cuda.is_available() else 'cpu')

def frechet_exp(chi, H, n_quad=8):
    theta = torch.linspace(0, 1, n_quad, device=chi.device)
    dtheta = theta[1] - theta[0]
    res = 0.
    for th in theta:
        E1 = torch.linalg.matrix_exp((1 - th) * chi)
        E2 = torch.linalg.matrix_exp(th * chi)
        res = res + E1 @ H @ E2
    return res * dtheta

def full_matrix_exp_diffusion(chi, epsilon):
    Delta_c = laplacian_of_exp(chi)          # weak form or spectral
    H = frechet_exp(chi, Delta_c)
    return epsilon * H

def free_energy(B, gamma, mu):
    trB = torch.diagonal(B, dim1=-2, dim2=-1).sum(-1)
    detB = torch.linalg.det(B)
    log_term = trB - 3. - torch.log(detB + 1e-30)
    quad = 0.5 * torch.sum((B - torch.eye(3, device=B.device))**2, dim=(-2, -1))
    return mu * ((1 - gamma) * log_term + gamma * quad)

def extra_stress(B, gamma, mu, a):
    I = torch.eye(3, device=B.device)
    S = (1 - gamma) * (B - I) + gamma * (B @ B - B)
    return 2 * mu * a * S

def asgs_residual_tet_gpu(U, mesh, beta, Wi, eta0, alphas,
                          lam, gamma, mu, a_param, kappa, epsilon=0.0):
    """
    Matrix-free ASGS residual on tetrahedral mesh.
    U : (ndof,) flattened [u(3), p, B(6)] on device
    Returns residual of identical shape.
    """
    u, p, B = unpack(U)
    eps   = 0.5 * (grad(u) + grad(u).transpose(-1, -2))
    Omega = 0.5 * (grad(u) - grad(u).transpose(-1, -2))
    tau   = extra_stress(B, gamma, mu, a_param)

    R_mom  = rho * (u · ∇)u - 2*beta*eta0*div(eps) - div(tau) + ∇p
    R_con  = div(u)

    # objective derivative + relaxation + optional diffusion
    material = advect(u, B)
    R_const = objective_derivative(B, grad(u), a_param, material) \
              + delta1*(B - I) + delta2*(B@B - B) \
              - kappa * laplacian(B)
    if epsilon > 0:
        R_const = R_const - full_matrix_exp_diffusion(log_map(B), epsilon)

    # ASGS fine-scale insertion
    R_mom   = R_mom   - alphas['mom']   * R_mom
    R_const = R_const - alphas['const'] * R_const
    return pack(R_mom, R_con, R_const)

def matrix_free_newton_gpu(U0, mesh, tol=1e-8, max_it=20, **params):
    U = U0.clone().to(device)
    for k in range(max_it):
        R = asgs_residual_tet_gpu(U, mesh, **params)
        if torch.norm(R) < tol:
            break
        def Jv(v):
            return (asgs_residual_tet_gpu(U + 1e-7*v, mesh, **params) - R) / 1e-7
        delta = gpu_gmres(Jv, -R, ...)
        U = U + delta
    return U
```

```python
@torch
.jit.script
def live_diagnostics_and_feedback(u, omega, tau, B, lam, gamma, mu,
                                  DeltaF, rho_neg, c_bkm=0.25):
    S = 0.5 * (grad(u) + grad(u).transpose(-1, -2))
    work = torch.sum(tau * S)
    ens  = 0.5 * torch.sum(omega**2)
    # kinematic production (diagnostic only)

    # thermodynamic free energy
    psi = free_energy(B, gamma, mu).sum()
    # weighted BKM increment
    wmax = torch.max(torch.abs(omega))
    B_w  = wmax / (1.0 + c_bkm * torch.abs(work) + 1e-8)
    # closed-loop injection into holographic / residual strata
    DeltaF  = DeltaF + 0.08 * work          # polymer work → free-energy jump
    rho_neg = rho_neg * (1.0 + 0.15 * work) # open soft channels where stress is high
    # optional: modulate scar-floor depth and Brockett ranking by E_act / psi
    return {
        'ens': ens, 'work': work, 'E_act': E_act, 'psi': psi,
        'B_w': B_w, 'prod_lambda': prod,
        'DeltaF': DeltaF, 'rho_neg': rho_neg
    }
```
