Odd viscosity \(\eta_o\) (also Hall or gyroviscosity) is a non-dissipative, parity- and time-reversal-breaking transport coefficient. It enters the stress tensor through antisymmetric components that do no work and produce no entropy. In the MHD residual layer it therefore survives as a persistent transverse force even after ordinary viscous and magnetic residuals have collapsed, modifying Alfvén/magnetosonic dispersion, suppressing small-scale intermittency, and supplying a clean additional monitor channel.

### 1. Stress-tensor and force contribution

In 2-D isotropic or quasi-2-D strongly magnetized geometry the odd contribution to the viscous stress is
\[
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}= \eta_o\bigl(\nabla\mathbf{u}^\perp+(\nabla\mathbf{u}^\perp)^\top\bigr)
\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad
\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}=\eta_o\nabla^2(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{u}),
\]
where \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) is the 2-D Levi-Civita tensor. In full 3-D Braginskii MHD the analogous gyroviscous pieces are the \(\eta_3,\eta_4\) terms aligned with the local magnetic unit vector \(\hat{\mathbf{b}}\):
\[
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^\wedge=\eta_3\,\mathbf{W}^{(1)\prime\prime}+\eta_4\,\mathbf{W}^{(2)\prime\prime}.
\]
These terms are added to the momentum residual exactly as the polymer stress \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) was added in the viscoelastic layer:
\[
\partial_t\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}+(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{B}+\dots
\]

Because \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}:\mathbf{D}=0\), the kinetic-energy identity remains unchanged; only the enstrophy and magnetic-enstrophy budgets acquire new non-dissipative cross terms.

### 2. Diagnostic functions keyed to odd-viscous decay characters

Live monitors extract instantaneous algebraic rates on rolling log-log windows:
\[
\begin{align*}
\alpha_o(t)&=\frac{d}{d\log(1+t)}\log\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}(t)\|_{L^2},\\
\alpha_\omega(t)&=\frac{d}{d\log(1+t)}\log\|\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\|_{L^2},\\
\alpha_B(t)&=\frac{d}{d\log(1+t)}\log\|\mathbf{B}(t)\|_{L^2}.
\end{align*}
\]
The three new residual diagnostics that close the odd-viscosity layer are
\[
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{D}_1^o(t)&=\bigl|\alpha_o(t)-\alpha_\omega(t)\bigr|,\\
\mathcal{D}_2^o(t)&=\frac{\|\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}\|_{L^2}}{\|\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd}\|_{L^2}+\delta},\\
\mathcal{D}_3^o(t)&=\frac{\bigl|\langle\boldsymbol{\omega},\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\rm odd})\rangle\bigr|}{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_{L^2}^2+\delta}.
\end{align*}
\]
All three are evaluated on-device after every accepted Newton step and written into the same diagnostic stream that already carries \(\mathcal{W}\), \(\mathcal{E}\), \(\mathcal{E}_{\rm AN}\) and the magnetic residual monitors.

### 3. Quantitative crossover and almost-hydrodynamic transition

Because odd viscosity is non-dissipative it does not decay by the same algebraic mechanism as the elastic remainder \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\). Its practical “switch” is therefore defined by the first time at which the ordinary viscous + magnetic residuals have already crossed over **and** the odd channel has settled into a pure wave-like regime:
\[
T_*^o=\inf\bigl\{t>T_*^{B}:\;\mathcal{D}_1^o(t)<10^{-3},\;\mathcal{D}_2^o(t)<10^{-2},\;\mathcal{D}_3^o(t)<10^{-3}\bigr\}.
\]
After \(T_*^o\) the expensive full Braginskii projectors may be frozen to a cheap gyroviscous monitor only; the residual becomes effectively Newtonian + odd-wave. The enstrophy formulation of the transition is simply the single scalar \(\mathcal{D}_3^o(t)\) above.
