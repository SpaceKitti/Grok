### 1. Constitutive correspondence and the formal limit

Oldroyd-B constitutive equation (paper eq. 9):
\[
\mathsfbi{T} + \tau\Biggl(
\partial_t\mathsfbi{T}
+ \boldsymbol{U}\cdot\nabla\mathsfbi{T}
-(\nabla\boldsymbol{U})^{\rm T}\cdot\mathsfbi{T}
-\mathsfbi{T}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}
\Biggr)
= \mu_{\rm p}\bigl(\nabla\boldsymbol{U}+(\nabla\boldsymbol{U})^{\rm T}\bigr).
\]

Redefined polymeric stress (eq. 14):
\[
\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}=\mathsfbi{T}+\frac{\mu_{\rm p}}{\tau}\mathsfbi{1}.
\]

Upper-convected evolution (eq. 17):
\[
\partial_t\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}
+ \boldsymbol{U}\cdot\nabla\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}
-(\nabla\boldsymbol{U})^{\rm T}\cdot\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}
-\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}
=-\frac1\tau\Bigl(\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}-\frac{\mu_{\rm p}}{\tau}\mathsfbi{1}\Bigr).
\]

Maxwell stress of ideal MHD (eq. 2, 14):
\[
\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm m}=\frac{\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}}{\mu_0},\qquad
\partial_t\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm m}
+ \boldsymbol{U}\cdot\nabla\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm m}
-(\nabla\boldsymbol{U})^{\rm T}\cdot\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm m}
-\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm m}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}
=\frac\eta{\mu_0}\bigl(\boldsymbol{B}\nabla^2\boldsymbol{B}+(\nabla^2\boldsymbol{B})\boldsymbol{B}\bigr).
\]

**Formal identity** (paper eq. 19):
\[
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\text{(Oldroyd-B)}
\;=\;
\lim_{\eta\to0}\text{(MHD)}.
\]

### 2. Multi-vector magnetic-like representation of polymeric stress

Paper eqs. 20–26 supply the exact isomorphism used by the hive:
\[
\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}=\frac1{\mu_0}\sum_{i=1}^3\boldsymbol{B}_i\otimes\boldsymbol{B}_i.
\]
Each \(\boldsymbol{B}_i\) obeys the induction-like equation
\[
\partial_t\boldsymbol{B}_i+\boldsymbol{U}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{B}_i
=\boldsymbol{B}_i\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{U}
-\frac1{2\tau}\Bigl(\boldsymbol{B}_i-\frac{\mu_0\mu_{\rm p}}{\tau}\boldsymbol{Q}_i\Bigr),
\]
where the dual frame \(\{\boldsymbol{Q}_i\}\) satisfies the algebraic constraint
\[
\frac12\sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(\boldsymbol{B}_i\otimes\boldsymbol{Q}_i+\boldsymbol{Q}_i\otimes\boldsymbol{B}_i\bigr)=\mathsfbi{1}
\]
(solved by the reciprocal vectors \(\boldsymbol{C}_i\) and an arbitrary antisymmetric part \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\)). Divergence of stress collapses to the Lorentz-like form
\[
\nabla\cdot\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}=\frac1{\mu_0}\sum_{i=1}^3\boldsymbol{B}_i\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{B}_i.
\]
In the hive the polymer residual is therefore replaced by three simultaneous induction residuals; the log-confinement and ASGS stabilisations act component-wise on the \(\boldsymbol{B}_i\).

### 3. MRI-equivalent instability and local dispersion relation

Basic flow (rotating plane Couette, paper eq. 41):
\[
\boldsymbol{U}=-2Ax\,\boldsymbol{e}_y,\qquad\Omega=\text{const}.
\]
Angular velocity decreases outward when \(A>0\). Linearised analysis yields the local dispersion relation (limit \(\tau\to\infty\), \(\nu=\eta=0\), \(k_z\gg k_x\)):
\[
\omega^2=\frac{4\Omega(\Omega-A)k_z^2+s^2(s^2-4\Omega A)}{k_z^2+\kappa^2},
\]
which is identical to the ideal magnetorotational dispersion relation upon the identification
\[
\frac{B_z^2}{\mu_0}\;\longleftrightarrow\;\frac{\mu_{\rm p}}{\tau}.
\]
(The maximal growth rate is exactly the Oort constant \(A\).) The instability extracts kinetic energy of the shear even when specific angular momentum increases outward; it is independent of streamline curvature and is therefore distinct from both the classical Rayleigh inertial mode and the purely elastic Couette instability (the latter has no MHD counterpart).

Hive monitors now evaluate the instantaneous growth rate estimator
\[
\gamma_{\rm MRI}(t)=\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{-\omega^2_{\rm max}(k;\,A(t),\Omega(t),\mu_{\rm p}/\tau)}
\]
on every accepted Newton step, together with the residual energy transfer
\[
\mathcal{W}_{\rm MRI}=\int\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\bigl(\nabla\cdot\mathsfbi{T}_{\rm p}\bigr)\,dV.
